A family farm in Normal, Illinois.

Aganze Growing Roots Farm is a small, family-operated farm that teaches youth entrepreneurship, sustainable agriculture and self-sufficiency.

Many children today rarely see how food is grown, or practice budgeting, business and caring for animals and the land. Our farm gives them a hands-on place to learn responsibility, stewardship and practical skills.

We believe young people can build good livelihoods where they grow up, and that training works best when it is rooted in the community. We join traditional knowledge with modern science to help youth grow strong futures.

Farming meets technology.

Good farming today uses information as well as hard work. Learners find out what each tool does, why it helps, and how to use it safely.

Fertilizer and soil

Soil tests and nutrient apps show what the soil really needs, so only the right amount is used. Learn how precision tools cut waste and protect water.

Pests and weeds

Scouting apps and photo identification spot problems early. Learn integrated pest management, and how targeted spraying, including GPS and drone tools, helps farmers work smarter.

Crops and water

Weather data, soil moisture sensors and timed drip irrigation help decide when to plant and water.

Livestock care

Digital records for animal health, feed, eggs and milk. Electronic tags, automatic waterers and feeders, and sensors or cameras that watch housing conditions.

Food preservation

Temperature monitoring and record keeping to store eggs and goat milk safely.

Farm business

Record apps, budgeting spreadsheets and online selling show how technology turns work into income.

Safety first. Young children learn the ideas and watch demonstrations. Adults handle all fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides and machinery.

Youth program: one year, three steps.

For young people aged 16 to 30. Applications open when the farm opens. Applicants can learn by doing, working with mentors and turning practical skills into income.

Months 1 to 4

Learn the basics

Soil, crops, water and harvest, on your own crop plot.

Months 5 to 8

Start a business

Plan an agri-business and use digital tools and climate-smart methods.

Months 9 to 12

Lead your community

Run a real community project and graduate with a certificate.

What you will learn.

Farming

Healthy soil, crop production, water management and storing food after harvest.

Technology

Farm apps, weather tools, mobile money and selling online.

Money

Pricing, record keeping, saving and writing a one-page business plan.

Leadership

Speaking up, solving problems together and running community projects.

Who can join.

  • Aged 16 to 30 (under 18: parent or guardian consent)
  • Living in a rural or peri-urban community
  • Able to read and write the basics, or willing to learn
  • Interested in farming, food, enterprise or community leadership

No exams or formal qualifications. Girls and young women, youth with disabilities, and vulnerable youth get priority.

What you get.

  • A small program fee on a sliding scale, based on what a family can afford
  • Hands-on training in farming, business and leadership
  • Mentorship and support from local adults and partners
  • A chance to grow practical skills and earn through the farm

Visitor safety rules

These rules keep children, adults and animals safe. We will confirm the details with each group when a visit is booked.

Before the visit

  • A teacher or group leader books the visit and brings enough adult chaperones. We will confirm how many when you book.
  • Please tell us in advance about allergies, health needs, or access needs for your group.

During the visit

  • Everyone signs in, stays with their group and follows the farm guide.
  • Children never go near animals, pens or equipment alone. Animals are touched only when supervised.

In an emergency

Our guides know where the first aid kit, exits and emergency numbers are. Follow the guide’s directions, and keep your group together. Adults should tell a guide immediately if someone is hurt.
